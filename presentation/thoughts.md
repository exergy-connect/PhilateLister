## 03 — Identification (core problem framing)

**Slide:** `03_slide_identification.png`

- Identification is not direct perception
- It depends on structured context
- Catalogs, conventions, expert systems define what counts as "same" or "different"
- Without structure, interpretation becomes unstable
- This is the space computers enter—and struggle with

**Transition:** First failure mode.

## 04 — Aspects of stamps

**Slide:** `04_slide_aspects.png`

Multiple dimensions coexist:

- Production (printing, paper, perforation)
- Usage (cancels, postal history)
- Variation (shade, errors, secret marks)
- Classification systems
- Provenance / annotation

- No single "true representation" of a stamp
- Identity is distributed across systems

**Key idea:** No single true representation—identity is distributed across systems.

**Transition:** Now show how ambiguity behaves in practice.

## 06 — Blue variety (Scott 233 vs 233a)

**Slide:** `06_slide_blue_variety.png`

* The distinction sounds simple: “blue” versus “ultramarine”
* In practice, color is unstable:

  * lighting
  * scanning
  * paper aging
  * ink variation
  * perception
* Computers can quantify color extremely well
* But philately is not asking for RGB values—it is asking for identity
* Introduce the PSAG story:

  * inherited website
  * old article by previous owner
  * rediscovered expertise shaping present appraisal
* The physical stamp emerged from an existing chain of interpretation
* Certification and reference copies stabilize meaning socially
* **Key idea:** Measurement alone does not produce philatelic identity

**Transition:** Now test AI on structured identification.
